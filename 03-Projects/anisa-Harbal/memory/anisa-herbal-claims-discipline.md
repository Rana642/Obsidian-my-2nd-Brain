The client's existing Meta-ad graphics and the halalveda.uk site they pointed
to as the reference both overclaim: "NO.1 HAIR GROWTH OIL", "Stops hair fall",
"grows new hair within 6 weeks", fake countdown timers, inflated
strike-through pricing, thousands of reviews. KNOWLEDGE_BASE.md §3 deliberately
softens these ("Reduces hair fall with regular use") to stay clear of Meta ad
policy.

**Why:** The site is meant to read pharmacy-adjacent, not Instagram-seller, and
an ad account ban would cost far more than the extra conversions. The client
supplies the aggressive material themselves, so the tension recurs.

**How to apply:** Take structure and conversion mechanics from the reference —
bundle-and-save selector, cart drawer, COD checkout, review layout — but never
the claims, fake urgency, or invented social proof. Two graphics
(`no1-hair-growth-oil-checklist.png`, `six-benefits.png`) are excluded from the
site for this reason; keep them out unless revised. See
[[working-style]] for how reference material tends to arrive.
